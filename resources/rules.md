## ORIGINAL RULES

A free surface of at least **120 × 150 cm** is required.

The deck is shuffled and **five cards are dealt to each player**, who will previously have chosen one of the four colors: **green, red, blue, or yellow**.

The cards are placed **face up** in front of each player.

The first player places a card in the center of the playing area. On their turn, each player may place one of their cards adjacent to **any edge of any card already in the playing area**, provided that the colors of the adjacent edges are always the same.

A **white** color can have **any** other color placed next to it.

Next to a **black** color, no other card may be placed.

Cards may only be placed in a **specific orientation**, determined by the design of the card (the figure must always remain **upright**).

A card may not be placed in isolation (except for the first card), nor may it touch another card only at a corner.

The objective of each player is to have, at the end of the game — **when the cards run out, with each player receiving a new card after each move** — the greatest number of **exposed semicircles of their chosen color** (that is, semicircles with no card adjacent to them).

Each semicircle is worth **one point** for its corresponding color, with the following exceptions:

* Cards that have a **star**: **3 points per semicircle**.
* A **crown**: **5 points per semicircle**.
* **White semicircles** do not count for anyone.
* **Black semicircles** count for the color that is **directly opposite** them.

If the opposite color (within the same card) is **white**, then the black semicircle counts for the color that is adjacent to the white semicircle **on another card**, if there is one, and is worth the same number of points (**1, 3, or 5**) as that color.

In the case of the **four Horsemen of the Apocalypse**, which have **three black semicircles and one white semicircle**, each of the black semicircles is worth whatever the semicircle of the color adjacent to the white one is worth.

When a player cannot play any of their five cards, they **pass**, and continue passing **without drawing new cards** until they can play a card. **This rarely happens.** 

If the player can play, they **must do so even if it is not advantageous to them**. Since the cards are face up, the other players can see whether or not a player is able to play.
